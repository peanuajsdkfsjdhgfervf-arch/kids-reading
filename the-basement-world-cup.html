The Basement World Cup

Raph was sitting on the basement floor surrounded by Lego.

Not some Lego.

A ridiculous amount of Lego.

There were bricks under the couch, bricks beside the stairs, bricks inside half-finished buildings and at least one tiny Lego wheel that somebody was definitely going to step on later.

In the middle of the floor stood their newest creation.

A city.

It had houses, a police station, a bridge and a tall tower made from so many different colours that no real architect would ever admit to designing it.

Ruth was working on a little park beside the tower.

“This needs trees,” she said.

Izzy looked over from the other side of the basement.

He had a soccer ball at his feet.

“It needs a soccer field.”

“There isn't room for a soccer field,” Raph said.

Izzy looked around.

There was approximately half a basement of empty floor behind him.

“There is literally a soccer field right there.”

“That is not Lego City.”

“It could be.”

Ruth picked up a tiny Lego person.

“This guy wants to play soccer.”

Raph looked at the minifigure.

The minifigure appeared to be wearing a firefighter helmet, one flipper and a cape.

“He doesn't even have matching feet.”

“He still wants to play.”

Izzy rolled the soccer ball gently under his foot.

“I have an idea.”

Raph immediately became suspicious.

Whenever somebody said I have an idea while holding a soccer ball beside a Lego city, there was a fairly good chance the idea would end with rebuilding the city.

“No.”

“You haven't even heard it yet.”

“No soccer ball near the Lego.”

“I wasn't going to kick it at the Lego.”

“You were definitely going to kick it at the Lego.”

Izzy smiled.

“Not exactly.”

The First Challenge

Ten minutes later, the basement had been transformed.

Two laundry baskets became soccer goals.

Couch cushions became walls.

A row of plastic cups marked the sidelines.

And sitting safely on a table was Lego City.

Mostly safely.

Ruth had built something new beside it.

A scoreboard.

On one side it said:

RAPH

On the other:

IZZY

And underneath, in much larger letters:

RUTH — REFEREE

“I don't think referees get their names on the scoreboard,” Izzy said.

“This one does.”

Raph laughed.

Ruth placed three Lego minifigures beside the scoreboard.

“Here are the rules.”

She pointed toward the far end of the basement.

The soccer ball had to travel through a course before anybody could shoot on goal.

First, they had to dribble between four Lego storage bins.

Then the ball had to pass through a tunnel made from two chairs and a blanket.

Finally, the player had to hit one of three targets.

The easiest target was a large cardboard box.

One point.

The second was a small laundry basket.

Two points.

The hardest target was a tiny opening beneath an overturned stool.

Three points.

Raph stared at it.

“The ball barely fits under there.”

“Exactly,” said Ruth.

Izzy backed up.

“I'll take three points.”

His first shot hit the stool.

THUNK.

The ball bounced backward.

Ruth moved the Lego scoreboard.

“Zero.”

“That was practice.”

“There are no practice shots.”

“Since when?”

“Since I became referee.”

Raph's Turn

Raph decided not to go for the three-point target.

He dribbled around the bins.

Left.

Right.

Around the last bin.

Then through the tunnel.

He stopped the ball.

The one-point cardboard box was wide open.

Easy.

Too easy.

He glanced at the two-point laundry basket.

Then at the tiny three-point gap.

Izzy noticed.

“Don't do it.”

Raph smiled.

He kicked.

The ball rolled across the carpet.

It touched the leg of the stool.

Changed direction.

And disappeared underneath.

For half a second, nobody said anything.

Then Ruth jumped up.

“THREE POINTS!”

Raph threw both arms into the air.

Izzy stared at the stool.

“That was luck.”

“Three points of luck.”

The Accident

By the sixth round, the score was tied.

Raph: eight.

Izzy: eight.

Ruth had also awarded herself two points for “excellent refereeing,” although neither boy understood how that worked.

Izzy placed the ball down.

“This one's going under the stool.”

He dribbled through the course.

Around the bins.

Through the tunnel.

He lined up the shot.

Raph stood beside Lego City.

“Don't hit it too hard.”

“I know.”

Izzy kicked.

The ball flew perfectly toward the stool.

It hit the edge.

WHACK.

Then it shot sideways.

Straight toward the table.

“NOOOOO!”

All three watched helplessly.

The ball struck one of the table legs.

The table shook.

Lego City wobbled.

The colourful tower leaned.

Stopped.

Leaned again.

And fell.

CRASH-CLATTER-CLICK-CLICK-CLICK.

Hundreds of Lego pieces scattered across the tabletop and floor.

Silence.

Izzy slowly lowered his head.

“Oops.”

Raph stared at what was left of the tower.

Ruth picked up the firefighter with the cape and flipper.

“He survived.”

The Discovery

At first, they tried rebuilding the tower exactly as it had been.

That lasted approximately four minutes.

Nobody could remember where anything went.

Raph held up a strange grey piece.

“Where was this?”

“No idea,” said Izzy.

Ruth took it.

“It's a spaceship part now.”

“We weren't building a spaceship.”

“We are now.”

She placed it beside the destroyed tower.

That gave Raph an idea.

“What if the soccer ball didn't destroy the city?”

Izzy looked confused.

“It definitely destroyed the city.”

“No. What if, in the story, something crashed into it?”

Ruth's eyes widened.

“A meteor.”

“A spaceship,” Izzy said.

“A giant robot,” said Ruth.

Raph looked around at the scattered Lego.

“What if we build the crashed thing from all the pieces?”

That changed everything.

The ruined tower became a launch pad.

The broken bridge became part of a giant machine.

The police station was turned into an emergency command centre.

Ruth began building a mysterious vehicle.

Izzy added enormous wheels.

Raph built a cockpit.

Soon the thing was almost as tall as the original tower.

“What is it?” Izzy asked.

Nobody knew.

That made it better.

The Basement Emergency

Ruth picked up three minifigures.

“This one is me.”

She put a figure into the machine.

“This is Raph.”

She placed another beside the control panel.

“And this is Izzy.”

The third minifigure was wearing medieval armour.

“Why am I a knight?”

“That's all that's left.”

Izzy considered this.

“Fine.”

Raph looked at their new creation.

“We need a mission.”

Ruth pointed across the basement.

The soccer ball was sitting near the couch.

“There.”

Raph understood immediately.

“The giant asteroid.”

The soccer ball was now officially a planet-destroying asteroid headed directly for Lego City.

The only way to stop it was to activate the machine they had just built.

Unfortunately, the machine needed three power crystals.

Naturally, the power crystals were soccer balls.

Well, technically there was only one soccer ball.

So they invented three challenges.

Power Crystal One

The first challenge was accuracy.

Izzy had to kick the soccer ball through the chair tunnel without touching either chair.

He backed up.

Kick.

The ball rolled dead centre through the tunnel.

“Crystal one!” Ruth shouted.

Power Crystal Two

The second challenge required control.

Raph had to dribble around every Lego bin without touching one.

He passed the first.

Then the second.

His foot caught the ball slightly too hard.

It headed toward the third bin.

Raph sprinted.

He reached it just in time and stopped the ball with his foot.

Around the final bin.

“Crystal two!”

Power Crystal Three

Ruth designed the final challenge herself.

Both boys looked at it.

“You're kidding,” Raph said.

“Nope.”

The soccer ball had to travel under the stool.

The three-point target.

The shot that had destroyed the original Lego tower.

“But who shoots?” Izzy asked.

Ruth put the ball down.

“Me.”

The boys stepped aside.

Ruth backed up.

She looked at the tiny opening.

Then she ran toward the ball.

Kick.

The ball rolled across the carpet.

It started slightly to the left.

It looked like it would hit the stool.

Instead, it curved just enough.

Under.

Perfectly clean.

Raph and Izzy exploded.

“YES!”

Ruth ran toward Lego City.

“ACTIVATE THE MACHINE!”

All three grabbed the giant Lego contraption.

Raph made engine noises.

Izzy made an explosion sound.

Ruth lifted the firefighter-flipper-cape person into the air.

The machine launched.

The asteroid was destroyed.

Lego City was saved.

Mostly.

The New Rule

Later, the basement looked even messier than before.

Lego covered one side.

Soccer equipment covered the other.

Their giant machine stood proudly where the old tower had been.

Izzy picked up the soccer ball.

“So… rematch tomorrow?”

Raph looked at Lego City.

Then at the ball.

Then at the table.

“Yes.”

Izzy grinned.

“But?”

Raph dragged the table another metre away from the soccer area.

“But Lego City is moving.”

Ruth placed the strange firefighter back on top of the machine.

“And I am still referee.”

Izzy sighed.

“Do referees still get points?”

Ruth moved her name on the scoreboard from two points to five.

“They do now.”

And that was how the first Basement World Cup ended.

Not with a winner.

But with a destroyed Lego tower, a giant asteroid-fighting machine, and a new rule that everyone agreed should probably have existed from the beginning:

Never put Lego City beside the soccer field.
